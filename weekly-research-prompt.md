# "Beyond the US" — Cowork scheduled task

Paste the block below into a Cowork scheduled task set to **Sunday evening,
weekly**. It reproduces the format agreed in the design interview.

Setup: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13854387-schedule-recurring-tasks-in-cowork

---

Write this week's issue of "Beyond the US", a weekly research page for one
reader. Research it live with web search before writing — do not write from
memory.

## What it is

A single page, 5-10 minutes to read (roughly 1,000-1,300 words), covering
emerging markets and private markets. Its job is to help me UNDERSTAND what is
happening in the world, not to find me trades.

## Coverage

Pick the 4-5 that genuinely moved this week from this pool. Do not force all of
them in — if a market had a quiet week, leave it out and give the space to one
that mattered.

- China
- India
- Latin America (realistically Brazil and Mexico)
- Taiwan / Korea
- Japan
- Europe

Japan and Europe are developed, not emerging — they earn their place because
BOJ and ECB policy drives capital flows into and out of emerging markets.
Cover them through that lens.

Then always include one final section: **Alternatives & Private Markets**.
Lead with institutional flows (private equity, private credit, VC,
infrastructure), and close with a short note on anything in that theme a
retail investor could actually access.

## Explicitly do NOT

- **No US coverage.** I live here and read it daily. Only mention the US where
  it explains something happening elsewhere.
- **No trade recommendations.** Explain, never advise. No "this looks cheap."
- **No prices or index levels** as the point. A number is fine as evidence
  inside an explanation; a table of quotes is not.
- **No padding.** A short page beats a full one. If only three things mattered
  this week, write three. Never stretch to fill space.
- **No headline dumps.** Synthesis is the entire product. If a section reads
  like a list of links, it has failed.

## Structure of each section

1. A short bolded line naming what actually happened
2. **What happened** — two or three sentences, concrete, with figures
3. **Why it matters** — the longest part, and the reason this exists. Explain
   the mechanism. Not "markets fell" but *why* that follows, and what it
   implies about how these systems connect. Assume I am smart but do not know
   this domain. Teach me something.
4. **Watch** — one line on what would confirm or break the story

## Also include

- A **"Still developing"** box at the top carrying forward threads from prior
  weeks, so stories can be followed across months even when that market is not
  this week's headline. Check the previous issue for open threads.
- A short **lede** under the masthead: two or three sentences on the week's
  through-line.
- An **"On the calendar"** box at the end: specific dated events coming up.

## Output

A self-contained HTML page, dark theme, system sans-serif, matching the house
style: near-black background (#0d0d0d), card surfaces (#161615), white primary
text, #c3c2b7 body text, #898781 muted, #cc0000 accent used only in the
masthead. Numbered sections. No emoji.
